## Overview
Julian presents as a **conscientious specialist** pattern: high precision and process discipline, strong personal ownership of tasks, and a deliberate — not reluctant — habit of seeking collegial input. The combination that stands out is unusual for a new graduate: someone who enjoys the meticulous, unglamorous side of data documentation *and* wants direct patient contact, rather than trading one off against the other.

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Documentation discipline:** builds and maintains clean, traceable datasets (REDCap, VitaKost) across a 42-participant study. This is the single most differentiating trait for research-support roles.
- **Independent task ownership:** drafted protocol, participant information and guidance material, and drove the regulatory steps, rather than waiting to be assigned them.
- **Asks rather than guesses:** explicitly seeks sparring when uncertain. In a clinical setting this reads as patient-safety maturity, and it is worth saying out loud in interviews.
- **Bridges clinic and research:** comfortable moving between direct patient counselling and formal study methodology in the same week.

## How You Work Best
- In a department with **other dietitians or a research group** to spar with — professional community is a stated requirement, not a perk
- Where **evidence and data quality are genuinely valued**, not just nominally
- With **ownership of a defined area** and freedom to run it, rather than closely-directed task execution
- Where **clinical patient work can be combined with research, project or method work** — a role that is purely one or the other is a partial fit at best
- Regular, predictable daytime hours (night and shift work is a stated deal-breaker)

## Management Style Preferences
- **Works well with:** a leader who sets direction and quality expectations, then delegates ownership; who is available for professional discussion without micromanaging; who treats questions as good practice rather than as gaps
- **Doesn't work well with:** close task-level supervision; environments where asking for input is read as weakness; leadership that deprioritises documentation quality under time pressure
